In plain language

France publishes every price change at every petrol station. I built a pipeline that loads a year of it and checks every single event before anything is counted. If an event looks wrong, it gets a label saying why; nothing is silently thrown away. To prove the checks work, I planted 586 known errors in real data and measured how many were caught. On top of the clean data sits a forecast that tells you whether to fill up today, but only on the days it is actually confident.

The question

Most pipeline demos show the happy path. The harder question is: how do you know your data is right, and how do you prove the checks themselves work? And once the data is trustworthy, can it support a real pricing decision, like "fill up today or wait?", without pretending to know more than it does?

What happens, step by step

  1. Load. The adapter streams the government XML, converts old millième prices to euros, attaches the Paris time zone and writes one file per day.
  2. Freeze. Each day becomes a Parquet file in bronze. A manifest stores row counts and checksums; a file that is not price data is refused.
  3. Judge. dbt unpivots every row into price events and runs six hard rules (quarantine) and two soft rules (flag), each with a reason code.
  4. Reconcile. A test checks that every bronze event is in silver exactly once, per day. Nothing can disappear quietly.
  5. Aggregate. An ASOF join finds the price in force at noon at every station; marts roll it up to country, département and city.
  6. Break it on purpose. Copies of 14 real days get 586 labelled faults; the gate is scored on what it catches and whether it gives the right reason.
  7. Forecast and abstain. Quantile models predict tomorrow's price; the band is calibrated on the last held-out week; the answer is "uncertain" unless the whole band is on one side of today's price.

What came out of it

French stations change each fuel's price about once a day, mostly overnight and in the morning; in November 2025, 86% of station-fuel-days had exactly one change. Diesel peaked on 22 September 2026 at a national average of 2.40 €.

Who moves first?

Leaders and followers come out in about equal numbers, median lead share 0.5. That is what chance would give when neighbours all reprice overnight, so I don't claim a leadership effect until a shuffled-time baseline says otherwise.

The fault catalogue was written alongside the rules, so this proves the rules are implemented correctly and raise almost no false alarms on real data. It does not prove they would find a kind of error nobody thought of.

Fill up today?

Day-to-day French prices are close to a random walk, so the point forecast is no better than "tomorrow = today". The calibrated band is honest, and "fill now" beats its base rate. "Wait" is wrong more often than right, so it should not be shown to users. That call was made after seeing this test window and still has to hold on the next months.

Decisions, and what they cost

Flag, instead of drop
Every event gets a verdict and a reasonBad rows stay visible and countable, and the reconciliation test can prove nothing vanished. It caught DuckDB's UNPIVOT silently dropping NULLs.
Cost: a bigger silver table and an extra test per day.
Same station type, instead of whole region
Motorway stations are compared with motorway stationsThe first full run flagged 20,418 events; 81% were the 477 motorway stations, which are expensive by design. After the change: 5,064.
Cost: smaller peer groups in rural départements.
Abstain, instead of always judging
Fewer than 5 comparable stations: no verdictHourly peer groups were often one or two stations, so outliers hid inside their own median: 68.8% recall. Day-level peers with a minimum: 97.5%.
Cost: some rural outliers are never judged.
Daily grain, instead of hourly
Match the grain to the marketAn hourly grid per city spilled about 2 GB per month on a 3 GB machine and added nothing where prices move daily. Hourly marts stay switchable for Germany.
Cost: no intraday analysis for France.
French open data, instead of waiting
Ship on the data you can getGermany's Tankerkönig history now needs a verified account and sign-ups were paused. France publishes everything openly. The adapter design keeps Germany one file away.
Cost: not the local market yet.
Abstain, instead of always forecasting
"Uncertain" is an answerThe model answers on 3.4% of days. When it says "fill now", it is right 92% of the time, against 72% for always filling now.
Cost: most days, no advice.

Still open

  • Precision on real anomalies: the 7,050 real flags need a hand-labelled sample.
  • Leader/follower needs a shuffled-time baseline before the roles mean anything.
  • The forecast was tested on one 8-week window that contained a price peak; "never show wait" must be confirmed forward.
  • Germany: add the Tankerkönig adapter once account access works, and switch the hourly marts on there.

Reproduce it

git clone https://github.com/omkar-k0/fuelcycle && cd fuelcycle
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && make setup
make data        # French archives -> canonical daily files
make all         # pipeline, gate eval, forecast backtest, page data
make test        # end-to-end tests on a synthetic sample (CI)
