fuelcycle · data engineering case study

Every fuel price change in France, on one laptop

A DuckDB + dbt pipeline over a year of the French government's fuel price feed. Every event passes a data-quality gate that is scored by injecting known faults. Daily marts show prices by region and city and which stations lead. A next-day forecast answers "fill up today?" only when it is sure.

When stations change prices

French stations change each fuel's price about once a day, mostly overnight and in the morning. Share of all E10 price changes by hour of day, and how many of them are rises.

Cheapest and most expensive cities

Diesel on the last day of data, cities with at least 10 stations reporting.

Who moves first

For every pair of stations within 2 km: when one changes its E10 price, does the neighbour move in the same direction within 12 hours, before the first one moves again? A leader is followed at least twice as often as it follows. Stations with no neighbour or fewer than 30 moves get no label.

"Fill up today?" A forecast that knows when it's unsure

LightGBM quantile models predict tomorrow's price for France and 135 cities, per fuel. The 80% band is calibrated on the most recent held-out week. If the whole band sits above today's price the answer is fill now, if it sits below it's wait, otherwise uncertain. Rolling-origin backtest over the last 8 weeks, retrained every week.

Data-quality gate

Every price event is accepted, flagged (kept, but not used by the marts) or quarantined, each with a reason code. A dbt test checks that bronze rows = silver rows, so nothing is dropped silently.

Fault injection: known faults are written into copies of 14 real days, then the gate is scored on whether it catches them and gives the right reason. Rows the gate already flags on the untouched copy are real-world anomalies whose truth is unknown, so they're reported separately and not scored.

What the real data changed

DuckDB's UNPIVOT silently drops NULLs.The bronze = silver reconciliation test failed on injected rows with an unreadable price. They were vanishing instead of being quarantined. Fixed with INCLUDE NULLS.
Motorway stations aren't outliers, they're expensive.In the first full run, 81% of all flags (16,522 of 20,418) hit the 477 motorway stations. Each station is now compared with peers of the same type in its region.
Peer groups that are too small hide outliers.Comparing within the same hour left many events with one or two peers, so the median was the event itself. Recall on injected outliers was 69%. Peers are now the whole day, and with fewer than 5 comparable stations the rule abstains: 97.5%.
An hourly grid doesn't fit a daily market.86% of station-fuel-days have one price change. An hourly grid per city spilled about 2 GB per month on a 3 GB machine and added nothing. Area marts are daily; the hourly marts stay switched on for German data.

Silver is a dbt incremental model built one month at a time, so memory stays flat. The French feed is converted into the same daily CSV layout as Germany's Tankerkönig feed, so a second country is one adapter, not a new pipeline.
